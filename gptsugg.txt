L’ÉTUDE — UI IMPROVEMENT PLAN

Goal
Make the interface feel like a fast revision/reference tool rather than a collection of oversized information cards.

Core design principle:
SCAN → PEEK → DEEP DIVE

The data architecture is already strong enough. The main improvement should now be how information is displayed, how much is visible at once, and how users move between related concepts.

1. USE A 3-LEVEL INFORMATION MODEL EVERYWHERE

LEVEL 1 — SCAN
Show only enough information for the user to identify whether the item is relevant.

Example verb row:
prendre · to take
3rd group · irregular · avoir
6 expressions · 8 tenses · 14 examples

Do not immediately show:
- all senses
- all stems
- all conjugations
- every usage note
- all sources
- all chapters
- too many badges

LEVEL 2 — PEEK
Click the row, chevron, or Quick View button.

Example:
prendre — to take, to catch
Past participle: pris
Future stem: prendr-

Common constructions:
- prendre une décision
- prendre sa retraite
- prendre le train

Common examples:
- Je prends le bus.
- J’ai pris le train.

Open full verb →

LEVEL 3 — FULL DETAIL
The full entity page contains everything:
- meanings
- all conjugations
- stems
- expressions
- examples
- grammar links
- usage notes
- sources
- chapters
- related entities

2. STOP MAKING EVERYTHING A CARD

Cards should be used mainly for:
- homepage modules
- important traps
- summary panels
- chapter overview
- highlighted concepts

Dense lists/tables are better for:
- 496 verbs
- 1,002 vocabulary items
- 557 expressions
- 284 grammar rules
- 1,011 examples
- exercises

Recommended pattern:
Dense table/list → expandable row / side peek → full detail page

3. VERB LIBRARY — MAKE IT DENSER

Recommended columns:
Verb | Meaning | Group | Past Participle | Auxiliary | Usage

Example:
prendre | to take | 3rd · irregular | pris | avoir | 12 expr · 18 ex
venir | to come | 3rd · irregular | venu | être | 8 expr · 14 ex
parler | to speak | 1st · regular | parlé | avoir | 4 expr · 11 ex

Clicking a row should:
- expand a quick view, OR
- open a right-side preview panel

The user should be able to scan many verbs without seeing huge cards.

21. FIVE CORE INTERACTION PRIMITIVES

1. Dense searchable lists/tables
2. Inline expansion or side-panel peek
3. Full entity pages
4. Natural cross-links
5. Progressive disclosure

22. VISUAL STYLE

Keep the existing academic/Oxford Blue design direction.

Do not spend most of the redesign effort on:
- new colors
- extra shadows
- decorative card layouts
- excessive gradients
- unnecessary animations

Focus on:
- better information density
- stronger hierarchy
- less overblocking
- fast peeking
- better cross-linking
- cleaner tables/lists
- contextual navigation
- fewer unnecessary badges
- smarter expandable sections

FINAL DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

L’Étude should feel like:
a searchable French knowledge atlas

rather than:
a digital textbook
or
a dashboard full of cards

The user should be able to:
SCAN quickly
→ PEEK without losing context
→ OPEN full detail only when necessary
→ MOVE naturally between verbs, grammar, tenses, expressions, vocabulary, examples, and chapters.

The master dataset is rich enough to support this approach. The UI should now focus on exposing that richness gradually rather than showing everything simultaneously.
